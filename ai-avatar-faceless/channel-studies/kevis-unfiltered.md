## The 11-step system

1. **Train your home feed to find an audience (~2:20).** Spare YouTube account; pretend to be the 50+/60+ viewer. Search what they'd search — his example: "The band trick that erases 20 years of stains". Click only videos that (a) are getting views (rejects a video with only 200 views) and (b) are faceless/AI-avatar channels — never personal-brand videos. Watch, refresh home feed, repeat until the feed is full of such content. Output: endless list of working niches.
2. **Generate seed lists with AI (~3:22).** Prompt: "Give me 20 video ideas and seed lists that an over 50 year old senior would watch on their YouTube account in their spare time." Iterate; verify results are faceless channels.
3. **Refine and lock ONE niche (~6:55–8:35).** Follow-ups: "Can you give me seed list topics that older people would watch? Trying to find what we can focus on rather than make videos for anything" → "What kind of content would they put on and watch? Go."
4. **Demographic arbitrage — steal a winning format from a similar niche (~8:35–13:00).** Do NOT copy direct competitors. Find a channel in a very similar niche / AI-avatar demographic space that either (scenario 1) is blowing up recently and consistently (e.g. 20 videos, 17 popped off) or (scenario 2) is a bigger channel pulling high views for a long time exploiting one format. "Format" = title structures + thumbnail styles + ideation patterns. Screenshot the winning channel's thumbnails/titles (including channel name), paste into ChatGPT agentic "walk mode" (NOT chat mode) with the transfer prompt: "Winning format in these screenshots attached, I've chosen the niche below. I want to transfer this winning format onto the niche [e.g. gardening / lawns / growing food] because I believe they have similar audience behavior." Build once, reuse forever.
5. **Mass-extract the winning channel's thumbnail/title data (~14:31–21:05).** The "game changer". "Grogbot" + a shared Google Docs prompt: paste the prompt, replace the placeholder with the winning channel's link, say "I want you to do all the 37 videos." The bot downloads all thumbnails, pulls every title, embeds the title into each thumbnail, saves a labelled folder. Rule of thumb: ~40–50 videos is enough; for 200–500-video channels: "sort by popular and grab the most popular 50 videos."
6. **Train an AI on the packaging (~21:33–23:07).** Upload the labelled thumbnails to ChatGPT ("Astra" model) in batches of 20 (media limit), in walk mode, with a "part two" prompt so it studies the title+thumbnail combinations — why certain thumbnails pair with certain ideas, how they title, how they make thumbnails. His batch reviewed in 29 seconds.
7. **Create the AI avatar (~23:23–24:03).** Screenshot/frame an existing AI avatar from a video → Higgsfield → prompt: make him look older, make this person look slightly different / a completely different person, seat him in the backyard/garden/front lawn → download the keeper = the channel avatar's face.
8. **Scripting (~18:52, 19:15–20:56).** Feed one idea into the "Head Start script writer" (Claude skill, exclusive to Head Start; setup tutorial on his channel). Choose duration; choose model — Opus 5.5 recommended best (demo used Sonnet). The skill does research and refuses to write if the idea isn't viable — "absolute cheat code". Target: minimum 15–20 min videos to start ("make the script 17 minutes long"); scale to 30-min and 1-hour as the channel grows.
9. **Generate the video with Vid Rush (~24:51–27:11).** Subscription; complete onboarding; set up a brand profile. Persona 1 or Persona 2 → "Use AI avatar" → select your avatar → "custom script". Strip script-writer placeholders via ChatGPT: "Remove all of the placeholders from this script and just leave out the VO part / leave the voice over part" → paste → save → start processing → approve the quote → ignore Vid Rush's own thumbnails → generate. Queue: done in ~1 hour. Countdown videos → "top 10 video" mode (~28:42); documentaries auto-select.
10. **Thumbnails on demand (~23:07–24:51).** With the trained prompt: drag the avatar image in + "Use the face attached as the avatar, as the channel avatar" + add the title from the other chat → ChatGPT's image model (GPT Image 2 — "one of the best image models") produces the thumbnail in the stolen winning format. Example thumbnail text: "You're throwing money away" — judged on curiosity and urgency.
11. **Review before publishing, then scale (~29:04–29:57).** "How those decisions connect. The audience helps us choose the subject... and the title and thumbnail and video all need to deliver the same promise." Batch-produce the first 60 days of video ideas; scales to 10+ channels (he runs 10+).

## Niche guidance

- Criteria: 50+/60+ demographics, evergreen demand, faceless/AI-avatar friendly, proven views on faceless competitors, one exploitable winning format, high RPMs.
- Named categories: physical safety (anti-burglar), health/physical fitness for 60-year-olds, food/recipes/cooking/old-fashioned recipes, home maintenance (plumbing, HVAC, low energy bills, avoiding expensive repairs), household tricks ("things you should never throw away", hidden uses for baking soda), avoiding scams/exploitation, retirement money, cars/mechanic maintenance, gardening/lawn care/growing food, history/war/crime/law/courtroom stories, RV/retirement lifestyle.
- Warned against: personal-brand-style content, copying direct competitors 1:1, "one-hit-wonder" niches (see his related video), copying his example niche blindly ("constantly evolving" — build strategy for whatever is working when you watch).

## Pitfalls (his "don't" list)

1. Don't copy video ideas 1:1 from direct competitors — the "wrong strategy" until the channel is big with audience trust.
2. Don't train the research feed on personal-brand or low-view videos.
3. Don't pull formats from unrelated entertainment (MrBeast-style) — stay inside the AI-avatar/older-demographic space.
4. Don't use ChatGPT's chat mode for heavy lifting — use walk/agentic mode.
5. Don't generate thumbnails inside Vid Rush — the trained prompt is better.
6. Don't chase highly-edited/polished content — the older audience doesn't want it.
7. Don't skip the pre-publish review — title/thumbnail/video must deliver the same promise.
8. Don't accept first prompt outputs — iterate prompts.
9. Don't scrape every video on huge channels — top 50 by popular suffices.
