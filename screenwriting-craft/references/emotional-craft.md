# Emotional Craft

How produced screenplays earn feeling without sentimentality — distilled from script reading and NYFA teaching.

## The core principles
- **Show, don't tell — the golden rule.** Concrete test: convey a problem by having someone *shout the fact* (amateur) vs. *showing the behavior* (a character hurriedly hiding a bottle). Same idea; only the second earns it. Assume the audience is more switched-on than you think.
- **Audiences connect to desire, not traits.** Empathy comes from shared *want* — even characters nothing like us land if their desire resonates. Emotion must ride on real conflict, never be stated.
- **Tears come from change, not events.** The audience cries at transformation (the u-turn, the choice, the kept promise) — not at the sad thing happening.
- **Grief is expressed through action and ritual.** Keeping a promise, carving a name, naming a child after the dead, carrying a worn object. The narration states plain facts; the audience supplies the emotion. Nobody announces their feelings.
- **Understatement.** Plain sentences, stated facts, no emotional adjectives. The flatter the telling, the harder the landing — because the audience does the feeling.

## Character engine: want vs. need
- **Want** = the conscious external goal. **Need** = the subconscious lesson. Their contradiction *is* the arc.
- Build on **ghost/wound + misbelief (the lie they believe) + flaw + fear**. Most of this never appears on the page — it surfaces through choices, gestures, dialogue.
- **True character is revealed by choices under pressure.** Plot is a machine for forcing choices.
- Every important relationship should **change** (beginning / middle / end). A flat relationship is a missed subplot.
- The two most powerful emotional levers (ScriptShadow): **unresolved authentic relationships** and **overcoming one's flaw** — handled realistically, never as melodramatic confrontation scenes.
- **U-turn check:** by the end, the protagonist wants the opposite of what they wanted at the start.

## Plants and payoffs
- **Bookends:** open and close on the same image/object, transformed. Planted casually early, returned at the climax carrying new meaning.
- **Object chains:** one object introduced innocuously (a hobby, a habit, a keepsake) → recurs with growing weight → pays off at the climax. In short form: plant it in minute one, pay it in the last 30 seconds.
- **Setup discipline:** if the third act has a problem, the real problem is in the first act. Weak resolutions are weak setups — fix beginnings.
- **Hide the machinery.** Audiences want familiar rhythms but must never see the formula. The beats may be predictable; the notes arrive as surprises.

## Endings
- **Opening Image / Final Image mirror:** end with a visual echo of the opening that proves transformation.
- **2-beat ending:** setup line + short quotable landing line (≤10 words). Keep the final spoken line tiny.
- **End on images, not speeches.** Then stop — "Don't hang around."
